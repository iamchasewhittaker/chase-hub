AI agents can now pay with your Visa right inside ChatGPT. The headlines are about the future of shopping. I keep landing on the part that never makes the press release: the dispute.

Here's the news. On June 10, Visa and OpenAI announced agentic payments. An AI agent can complete a purchase for you using a tokenized Visa credential bound to that specific agent, with spending caps and approval rules, and Visa says it handles the fraud and chargeback side.

I spent 7 years getting merchants live at Authorize.Net and CyberSource, then helping them when payments broke. The happy path was never the hard part. The dispute was. A customer calls and says "I didn't buy that." Now the buyer is an agent acting on someone's behalf. When the agent orders the wrong item, or orders it twice, who eats the loss? The merchant who shipped, the user who set it loose, or the company that built the agent?

The token-bound-to-an-agent piece is genuinely the right call. Real progress, not hype. But "Visa handles chargebacks" is one line in a press release and a year of edge cases in real life.

For builders, the quiet unlock is agents buying their own API and compute within limits. That part I'm excited about.

If an agent buys the wrong thing on your card, who should be liable?
